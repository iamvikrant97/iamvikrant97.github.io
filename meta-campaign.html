What I read from the numbers

Running a campaign is easy. Knowing what the result means is the job. Here is what the data actually told me.

  • It beat the benchmark. Meta's own comparison put my cost per profile visit 19% below similar ad sets running in the same window. At ₹0.36 a visit on a ₹1,270 budget, the spend worked harder than the peer median. That is the number I am proudest of, because it is not self-reported.
  • The ad reached mostly men, but women converted cheaper. 72% of reach was male, yet women came in at ₹0.33 per result against ₹0.39 for men. The algorithm spent more finding men, but women were the more efficient audience. Weighting the next budget toward women is a clear lever to lower cost per result.
  • The video lost people early. Roughly 16,500 three-second plays but only around 1,000 ThruPlays. People started watching and did not finish. The front of the video earned the click, the middle did not hold.
  • What I would test next. A tighter lean toward the audience that converted, and two or three different video openings to hold viewers past three seconds. That is where the cost per result gets cheaper, not by spending more, but by spending smarter.
Honest note. This was a small self-funded test, not a client budget. I ran it to prove I can set up, launch, read and optimise a live campaign with real money, using creative I made myself. The spend is small. The process is the same one a brand budget runs on.
